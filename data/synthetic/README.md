# Synthetic demo data (v3 showcase cohort)

`data/synthetic/` holds the synthetic data the app runs on in `BILUN_DATA_MODE=synthetic_demo`: the 16 patients (42 bills) of the v3 showcase cohort, the recipes, pathways, fault registry and reference tables they are built from, and the checks that guard them. Everything here is synthetic or public reference data. Nothing comes from a real patient or a real claim.

The cohort is built from the 42 showcase recipes (one per bill) in `data/synthetic/cohort/showcase_recipes.yaml`, the clinical presentation context in `data/synthetic/cohort/showcase_clinical.yaml`, the care pathways in `data/synthetic/pathways/*.yaml` (and `data/synthetic/faults/siblings/*.yaml`), and the fault registry in `data/synthetic/faults/fault_registry.yaml`.

> **Generated files — do not edit by hand.** Every Parquet table under `sut/claims/` and `sut/clean/`, and `cohort_registry.json`, is built deterministically by `scripts/synthetic/cohort/build.py`. Change the recipes, pathways, faults or reference tables and rebuild; `build --check` fails when a file differs from a fresh build.

A recipe is one bill: `patient_no`, `encounter_no` (1, 2, 3 ...; it defaults to 1), `pathway`, an optional `fault`, optional `medula_events` (see "MEDULA rejection events" below) and optional `pins` (any of `story`, `sex`, `age`, `admit`, `nights`, `span`, `include`, `course_days`, `chronic_health_report`, laid over the pathway's own `showcase` block for this bill). The recipes are listed by patient and then by encounter, without gaps. What follows from it:

- **Ids follow the build order, tables follow the dates.** The claim and episode ids carry `encounter_no` (`F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0004-002` is the second recipe of patient 4), so adding a bill later never renumbers another. The tables are laid out by patient and then by the date of the bill (the claim sequence breaks a tie): a bill dated before the patient's first bill has the later id and the earlier row. The three clean tables follow the same order (the lab rows are in the order of the feed contract: patient, line date, line id, parameter).
- **One patient, one profile.** Sex and birth year are the patient's: every bill of a patient has the same sex and the same year of birth (`recipe.patient_identity`; on the tables `patient.age_arithmetic`). There is one copay profile per patient, with the birth year of the first encounter.
- **One story per bill.** A story id belongs to one bill (`recipe.story`); a bill of a pathway that is already in use names a story of its own in its `pins`. A role of the registry names the bill of its story, and a story after the eighth is a scenario with that bill's claim.
- **Effective pins.** The pins of a bill (the pathway's own with the recipe's overrides) travel on the composed claim (`ComposedClaim.pins`); the lab values, the anadata age and sex and the prescription days read them, not the pathway.
- **Clinical entries per bill.** `showcase_clinical.yaml` has an entry per encounter (`patient_no` and `encounter_no`, the latter 1 by default). The vitals band (infant or adult) is the patient's, read from the first encounter's age.
- **Lab values.** A value is drawn from a hash of `patient|code|parameter|occurrence|salt`; the salt of the first bill is 0, 1 ... (so a one-bill patient's values are what they always were) and that of bill *n* starts at `1000 * (n - 1)`, so the same test on two bills of one patient gives two values.
- **Fault records.** A bill with a fault has an injection record and an answer-key row on its own claim; a bill without one is a clean bill and has neither.
- **Counts.** A bill is a claim header and an `anadata_clean` row (42); a patient is a copay profile (16). Test and script code that means patients counts profiles (or the encounters grouped by patient), never `anadata` rows.

## MEDULA rejection events

A recipe may list the MEDULA rejections of its bill (`medula_events`, empty by default): each names the line it is about by the pathway entry id (`entry_id`, an id of the pathway, or of the fault's sibling, whose expansion is the committed bill), the stage (`medula_stage`: `provizyon`, `hizmet_kayit` or `fatura_kayit`), the four-digit result code (`sonuc_kodu`), an optional error code (`hata_kodu`) and the message (`hata_mesaji`), with `basis` and `source` like every pathway entry. The builder resolves the entry to the line of the committed claim and writes one row of `medula_rejection_events.parquet`: the id `RED-<claim>-NN` (NN counts the events of the claim), the claim, patient and episode, the line (`related_line_type` and `related_line_id`; the legacy `related_procedure_code` and `related_procedure_line_id` for a procedure line) and the date of the line as `rejection_timestamp` (the loader wants it inside the claim's dates). The check `recipe.medula_event` refuses an event whose entry is not exactly one billed line of the committed bill (a line the fault takes out, a line a day, a performed-log line) and an event given twice. A clean twin carries no event: a rejection is what happened to the bill as it was submitted. The one event of the cohort is the 0901 of S10; whether a hospital would see that text for a manual haematocrit beside a hemogram is for the billing officer to confirm.

## Regenerate and check

Regenerate the files in `data/synthetic/` (the tables in `sut/claims/` and `sut/clean/`, and `cohort_registry.json`):

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.build
```

The output directory is `--out DIR` and defaults to `data/synthetic`. Verify that the committed files match an in-memory build by content (table frames and `cohort_registry.json`):

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.build --check
```

Two builds with the same Polars version write the same bytes, so a rebuild leaves every tracked file unchanged. Another Polars version may lay the same tables out in other Parquet bytes; `--check` compares by content, so it does not fail on that.

## Coverage metrics

Which of the 20 billing-report blocks and which of the 528 Wave A rule ids do the patients light up? `scripts/synthetic/cohort/coverage_metrics.py` runs the engine's patient report over a claims directory, applies the metric map of `plans/data/2026-10-20-demo-coverage-first-plan.md` section 9.2 (blocks with output, Wave A rule ids evaluated / matched / with a row, finding kinds), writes the numbers per patient and for the cohort as JSON, and prints the difference from an earlier file:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.coverage_metrics --out /tmp/before.json
# ... add a story, rebuild the cohort ...
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.coverage_metrics --out /tmp/after.json --compare /tmp/before.json
```

`--claims-dir DIR` measures another claims folder, `--patients ID ...` only those patients. On the first bills of the 8 patients S1 to S8 it reported 9 of 20 blocks with output, 273 of 528 rule ids evaluated, 14 matched and 14 with a row (the plan's baseline); with the two outpatient bills of patient 4 the same 8 patients report 286 evaluated, 16 matched and 15 with a row, and the whole cohort 15 blocks, 288 evaluated, 19 matched and 18 with a row (patient 12 adds the blocks `lab`, `procedure_addon_uplift` and `medula_rejection_playbook` and the matched ids `GENEL CERRAHİ-2` and `tablo15_12_duplicate_test`; `tests/unit/synthetic/test_coverage_metrics.py` pins these). On the committed cohort the app path shows 12 rate corrections (2 on each of S3 to S6 and S9, 1 on S1, and S2's waiver row), five patients with an EK-1/A match and no known report (`partial_exempt`: S3 to S6 and S9), three exempt patients (S2 on EK-1/A with its report, the two emergency patients on the visit type), and the same blocks and Wave A rule ids as the claims-only run (before S2's report and its control, WP8: 13 rate corrections and six partial patients, S2 among them).

## Phase notes

- **Clinical clean tables (`sut/clean/`):** `anadata_clean.parquet` (41 rows, one per bill), `recete_clean.parquet` (25 rows), and `lab_clean.parquet` (381 rows) are populated deterministically from the showcase claims and clinical registry.
- **Lab results (`sut/clean/lab_clean.parquet`):**
  - One result set per billed EK-2B lab line (17 codes, `parametre` names the result parameter): 381 rows. Patients 13 and 14 have none because their bills are packages (S20's day case too), patient 8 only the CRP of her ER visit (S49, the faulty line of that bill; her two stays are packages), patient 5 only the blood count and the creatinine of her ER visit (S41; her two stays are packages), and patient 11 (the newborn, S12) has none because the lab reference ranges are adult; cultures and serology are not L-codes and have no rows.
  - Values come from `data/synthetic/reference/lab_reference.csv` by a deterministic SHA-256 draw of `patient|code|parameter|occurrence|salt` (no random seed, no clock): the central 30 to 70 percent of the sex-specific limits, or 20 to 80 percent of the range of a state.
  - The per-patient states in `data/synthetic/cohort/showcase_clinical.yaml` are judgment (clinician review in issue #309). Repeat samples inside a state are independent draws: there is no automatic trend.
  - Coherence rules: the five differential counts sum to WBC (R1); RBC = HGB·10/MCH, HCT = MCV·RBC/10, MCHC = HGB/HCT·100 (R2); creatinine is inverted from a target eGFR and eGFR is recomputed with CKD-EPI 2021 (R3); HCO3 = 0.0307·pCO2·10^(pH−6.1), BE = 0.93·(HCO3−24.4+14.8·(pH−7.4)) and SaO2 by Severinghaus from pO2 (R4).
  - `anormal_mi` is true outside the sex-specific limits of the CSV (troponin I: M 34.2, F 15.6 ng/L) and for a strip grade other than `Negatif` (R5).
