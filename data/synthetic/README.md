# Synthetic demo data (v3 showcase cohort)

`data/synthetic/` holds the synthetic data the app runs on in `BILUN_DATA_MODE=synthetic_demo`: the 15 patients (26 bills) of the v3 showcase cohort, the recipes, pathways, fault registry and reference tables they are built from, and the checks that guard them. Everything here is synthetic or public reference data. Nothing comes from a real patient or a real claim.

The cohort is built from the 26 showcase recipes (one per bill) in `data/synthetic/cohort/showcase_recipes.yaml`, the clinical presentation context in `data/synthetic/cohort/showcase_clinical.yaml`, the care pathways in `data/synthetic/pathways/*.yaml` (and `data/synthetic/faults/siblings/*.yaml`), and the fault registry in `data/synthetic/faults/fault_registry.yaml`.

> **Generated files — do not edit by hand.** Every Parquet table under `sut/claims/` and `sut/clean/`, and `cohort_registry.json`, is built deterministically by `scripts/synthetic/cohort/build.py`. Change the recipes, pathways, faults or reference tables and rebuild; `build --check` fails when a file differs from a fresh build.

## Several bills of one patient

Seven committed patients have several bills: patient 15, whose stay for decompensated cirrhosis (S18, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0015-001`) is followed by a readmission for a variceal rebleed (S24, `encounter_no: 2`; see "Decompensated cirrhosis: albumin, then a variceal rebleed" below), patient 14, whose month of haemodialysis (S17, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0014-001`) is followed by an emergency session (S23, `encounter_no: 2`; see "A month of haemodialysis and an emergency session" below), patient 7, whose ER visit (S7, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0007-001`) is followed by an observation of one date and a follow-up (S47 and S48, `encounter_no: 2` and `3`; see "A stay of one date and the follow-up after it" below), patient 2, whose stay (S2, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0002-001`) is followed by a control visit (S32, `encounter_no: 2`; see "The control visit with the chronic health report" below), a readmission through the ER and the control after it (S33 and S34, `encounter_no: 3` and `4`; see "The readmission through the ER and the control after it" below), patient 10, whose ICU bill (S11, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0010-001`) is followed by the ward bill S14 (`F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0010-002`, recipe `encounter_no: 2`; see "The ward bill after an ICU bill" below), patient 12, whose ER visit (S10, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0012-001`) is followed by an admission (S22, `encounter_no: 2`; see "The ER visit and the admission after it" below), and patient 4, whose stay (S4, `F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0004-001`) is followed by a control visit (S39, `encounter_no: 2`) and a second visit (S40, `encounter_no: 3`; see "The outpatient bills after a stay" below). The builder supports more (tests build such cohorts in memory from `tests/fixtures/synthetic_encounters.py`, with one more bill on patients 4 (its fourth) and 7 (its fourth, a precursor dated before its first), and in the lab and prescription tests a second COPD stay of patient 2 (its fifth)). A recipe is one bill: `patient_no`, `encounter_no` (1, 2, 3 ...; it defaults to 1), `pathway`, an optional `fault`, optional `medula_events` (see "MEDULA rejection events" below) and optional `pins` (any of `story`, `sex`, `age`, `admit`, `nights`, `span`, `include`, `course_days`, `chronic_health_report`, laid over the pathway's own `showcase` block for this bill). The recipes are listed by patient and then by encounter, without gaps. What follows from it:

- **Ids follow the build order, tables follow the dates.** The claim and episode ids carry `encounter_no` (`F-SENTETIK-HASTA-0004-002` is the second recipe of patient 4), so adding a bill later never renumbers another. The tables are laid out by patient and then by the date of the bill (the claim sequence breaks a tie): a bill dated before the patient's first bill has the later id and the earlier row. The three clean tables follow the same order (the lab rows are in the order of the feed contract: patient, line date, line id, parameter).
- **One patient, one profile.** Sex and birth year are the patient's: every bill of a patient has the same sex and the same year of birth (`recipe.patient_identity`; on the tables `patient.age_arithmetic`). There is one copay profile per patient, with the birth year of the first encounter.
- **One story per bill.** A story id belongs to one bill (`recipe.story`); a bill of a pathway that is already in use names a story of its own in its `pins`. A role of the registry names the bill of its story, and a story after the eighth is a scenario with that bill's claim.
- **Effective pins.** The pins of a bill (the pathway's own with the recipe's overrides) travel on the composed claim (`ComposedClaim.pins`); the lab values, the anadata age and sex and the prescription days read them, not the pathway.
- **Clinical entries per bill.** `showcase_clinical.yaml` has an entry per encounter (`patient_no` and `encounter_no`, the latter 1 by default). The vitals band (infant or adult) is the patient's, read from the first encounter's age.
- **Lab values.** A value is drawn from a hash of `patient|code|parameter|occurrence|salt`; the salt of the first bill is 0, 1 ... (so a one-bill patient's values are what they always were) and that of bill *n* starts at `1000 * (n - 1)`, so the same test on two bills of one patient gives two values.
- **Fault records.** A bill with a fault has an injection record and an answer-key row on its own claim; a bill without one is a clean bill and has neither.
- **Counts.** A bill is a claim header and an `anadata_clean` row (26); a patient is a copay profile (15). Test and script code that means patients counts profiles (or the encounters grouped by patient), never `anadata` rows.

### The ward bill after an ICU bill (S14)

The ICU bill of patient 10 (S11) runs 2026-06-02 to 06-07; the ward bill (S14) is admitted on 06-07, the day the ICU bill ends. EK-2B 510090 covers yemek, yatak and vizit, so that day's bed is paid by the ICU bill, and the engine says so in two places: the ward bed on that day is a double billing (the Wave A rule `yatak_icu_overlap_510010`, a financial row with the 510010 line as its observed amount, 222.36, and no confirmed amount), and the expected bed days of the ward bill lose it (`yatak_puani`). The clean twin therefore bills 510010 on 06-08 to 06-10 only (pathway `urosepsis_ward_stay_after_icu_transfer`, flag `arrives_from_icu`) and the faulty bill also on 06-07 (the fault `use_sibling_pathway`, the sibling `urosepsis_ward_stay_transfer_day_billed`). The ward bill has no lab, drug or material line: a cross-bill rule reads the whole history, so a test the ICU bill already bills would be a duplicate within seven days. Read alone, without the ICU bill, the clean ward bill looks like a missing bed day (`yatak_puani`, 222.36): the proof and the clean-twin fixtures lay the twin over the loaded bill, with the ICU bill as history.

### The outpatient bills after a stay (S39, S40)

Patient 4 (S4: a woman of 86 with Alzheimer's disease, an itemized stay for acute pyelonephritis with delirium, 2026-05-11 to 05-16) has two outpatient bills in internal medicine, the first visits of the committed cohort. A visit has the claim type `poliklinik` (the visit grammar: `poliklinik` and `gunubirlik` join `acil` as claim types without a stay in the pathway schema), one date, no admission and no discharge. The control S39 (2026-05-25, nine days after the discharge) bills the examination 520030, a blood count, creatinine, a urinalysis and a urine culture; it is a clean bill: no fault, no injection record, no answer-key row (its story has a clean golden and no faulty one, `CLEAN_ONLY` in the proofs' `stories.py`, and no proof). The second visit S40 (2026-06-01, a week later, a low-grade fever, a urinary ultrasound) is the second outpatient application to the same branch within 10 days: SUT C000573 pays only the EK-2/A-2 procedures of such an application and not the examination fee, so the faulty bill carries the examination 520030 and its clean twin does not (the fault is `use_sibling_pathway`, the sibling is `pyelonephritis_dementia_second_visit_exam_billed`; the twin has the same date, the same header and the same diagnoses, as every clean twin). The engine finds the fault with the control as history (`detect_duplicate_exam_10day`, Tablo 15 #2) and reports it as a compliance finding without money. All three bills have the principal diagnosis N10 and their diagnoses are among N10, G30.1 and I10 and the stay's F05.1, so the patient's list of diagnoses is the one of the stay; the second visit bills no test, because a test of the control would be a duplicate within seven days. A cross-bill rule reads the whole history: the proof and the clean-twin fixtures give each bill the patient's other bills as history, and the report of the stay itself does not move: a later bill is history of an earlier one and adds nothing to its report (the proof of S4 stays what it was).

### The control visit with the chronic health report (S32)

Patient 2 (S2: a man of 81 with COPD, an itemized stay for an exacerbation with pneumonia, 2026-03-09 to 03-15) comes back to chest diseases (`Göğüs Hastalıkları`, EK-2A 1171) on Monday 2026-04-06, 22 days after his discharge: a `poliklinik` bill with the examination 520030 and no test, with the diagnoses J44.0 and I10 of the stay (so his list of diagnoses stays the one of the stay). It is a clean bill: no fault, no injection record, no answer-key row (`CLEAN_ONLY` in the proofs' `stories.py`, a clean golden, no proof).

Two things are new with it. **His chronic health report is on file**: both pathways of the patient pin `chronic_health_report: true` (every bill of a patient carries the same value, `recipe.patient_identity`), so his copay profile row has `has_chronic_health_report` true, and the EK-1/A exemption (SUT 1.8.5.(7): a specialist examination for a listed chronic disease, here J44 and I10-I15, with a health report) waives his share of the examination instead of staying partial. **The examination carries the patient's share** (slice G5b): a pathway line may carry `copay: {rate, basis, source}`, the composer then sets `copay_rate_applicable` and `copay_rate` on that line and bills it in full with `expected_payable_try` = billed x (1 - rate), half up to the cent, and the header's payable total follows its lines. The demo models the share as 20 %: billed 85.73, payable 68.58, and the 17.15 between them is what EK-1/A waives. SUT 1.8.1 sets the visit copay as a fixed amount per visit (C000304), not a rate; the rate is how the engine reads a line's share (`line_decomposition.py`), and whether MEDULA records the visit copay on the examination line this way is for the billing officer to confirm. The cohort check of the tables accepts a payable below the billed amount on such a line only (`money.payable_equals_billed`: the flag and a rate above 0 and below 1 go together).

What the app shows: on the app path (the cache's copay exemption) patient 2 moves from `partial_exempt` to `exempt`, his copay block holds the waiver row of the examination (`copay_exemption_ek1a`) and an exemption review, and loses the partial row and the generic 1.8.2 drug-copay row (the engine drops that row for any exempt patient, although EK-1/A waives 1.8.1 only: EI-25 of the plan). The claims-only run, the bill-level reports of S2's stay and every other patient do not move. The waiver is a patient benefit, not a fault: none is registered for it.

### A month of haemodialysis and an emergency session (S17, S23)

Patient 14 (a man of 71, born 1955) has end-stage kidney disease (N18.0) with the anaemia of chronic kidney disease (D63.8) and secondary hyperparathyroidism (E21.1), on chronic haemodialysis in nephrology (`Nefroloji`, EK-2A 1062; Z49.1, Z99.2). His sessions of May 2026, Monday, Wednesday and Friday from 05-04 to 05-29, are one `gunubirlik` claim over those dates (the first committed session claim with a `span`: the claim ends on admit+25, without admission and discharge): twelve lines of the EK-2C session package P704230 (SUT 2.4.4.D-1, C000963). The session price includes the tests, the drugs of a complication of the session, heparin and the day bed (C000969, C004117), so the claim bills none of them; the pathway grammar lets a bill of haemodialysis session packages only carry a drug line beside them (`package.mode`; the session packages have no EK-2C group and no package window, `package.window`, both listed in `reference/code_sets.yaml` as `dialysis_packages`). Beside them the claim bills his erythropoiesis-stimulating agent, darbepoetin (ARANESP 40 mcg, one box of four syringes, 40 mcg a week; price 0 as every drug), on a nephrology report of one month: its drug line carries `drug_specialty_board_report_present`, the report's duration `drug_report_duration_months: 1` (slice G9: a drug flag of the pathway since this story) and `prescriber_specialty`. The route of the product (its TİTCK name states none) is declared in `drug_routes`: given through the dialysis line, `Intravenöz`.

- **S17, the month**: the faulty bill names general surgery as the darbepoetin's prescriber (`set_flag`); SUT 4.2.9.A-1.(2) (C004193) lets nephrology, internal medicine, paediatrics or a dialysis-certified physician prescribe it. The engine's drug-indication rule for an ESA in chronic kidney disease (`4.2.9.esa_ckd_iron_hb_nephrology_report`, selected because the line carries the one-month report) finds it: a `prescriber_specialty_mismatch` violation and action, without money. The clean bill's line is `eligible`, the cohort's first drug line whose encoded rule applies and holds.
- **S23, the emergency session** (2026-06-12, a clean bill): a raised potassium (E87.5) brings him in out of rhythm for a session given as an emergency, a stay of one date (`nights: 0`) billed as the package P704210 alone (C000963). The package includes the tests of the emergency and the day bed (C000969): the bill has no bed, test or drug line, and the engine's bed-day motor asks for no day bed on it.

Whether SGK takes a month of sessions as one takip is not verified; the billing officer confirms it. The checklist of the engine names a documentation row for a "surgery" on both bills, because any EK-2C code counts as a surgical package there (EI-16 of the coverage plan).

### Decompensated cirrhosis: albumin, then a variceal rebleed (S18, S24)

Patient 15 (a man of 63, born 1963) has decompensated cirrhosis (K74.6). On Monday 2026-06-15 he is admitted to internal medicine (`İç Hastalıkları`, EK-2A 1000) with ascites and a melena (K92.2) for four nights: the ward bed 510010 on every calendar day, the companion 510121 on the four nights, a blood count and a serum albumin (L100320, a billed lab code since this story) on the admission day, and from day 2 human albumin, one 100 ml bottle of 20 % a day for three days (HUMAN ALBUMIN OCTAPHARMA, price 0 as every drug, `drug_inpatient_supply` true: supplied during the stay). His albumin is 2.2 g/dl: the clinical file gives it the state `cirrhosis_at_infusion_threshold` (1.8 to 2.5 g/dl, a state of the albumin row of `lab_reference.csv` since this story), so the value always lies at or under the 2.5 g/dl of SUT 4.2.27.Ç.(1) (C006389). On Monday 07-13, 28 days after the discharge, he comes back with bleeding oesophageal varices (I85.0, with his K74.6), hypotensive and tachycardic, for three nights: two red-cell units (705371) on arrival, one line per unit, and a blood count on arrival and on day 2. The endoscopy of either bleed is not billed: the EGD codes trigger the engine's add-on rule `pairing.701540_701410`, which cannot know that no biopsy was taken (EI-11 of the coverage plan). His list of diagnoses is K74.6, I85.0 and K92.2. Each bill has its own fault:

- **S18, the albumin stay**: the clean drug line carries the specialist report (`drug_specialty_board_report_present` true); the faulty bill bills it without (`set_flag`). The engine's albumin rule (`4.2.27.C.albumin_inpatient_3day_report`, selected by K74 and the inpatient supply) reads the report as required (SUT 4.2.27.Ç.(2), C006390: a report with the daily dose and the albumin level, at most three days' dose) and reports a `missing_board_report` violation and action without money, and the submission checklist a readiness row for MEDULA's 0612 (`claim_readiness.drug_board_report_missing`). The clean line is `eligible`. Whether SGK asks this report when the hospital supplies the albumin itself, rather than paragraph (1) alone (the level and the epicrisis), is for the billing officer to confirm.
- **S24, the rebleed**: SUT 4.6.(3) (C007926) pays an EK-2B blood component when the epicrisis names it, its count and its ISBT 128 blood-barcode numbers. The clean bill carries one synthetic number per unit (`isbt128_barcode`, a line flag of slice G5a; a facility code that does not exist); the faulty bill carries none (`set_flag` on both units). The engine reports a compliance finding per unit (`blood_component_isbt128_barcode`, no amount) and its Wave A rule of the same id is evaluated and matched for the first time in the cohort.

The cohort draws a haemoglobin of about 9 g/dl on both bills (8.6; 8.9 on arrival and 8.8 after the two units): the generator keeps the red-cell indices consistent, which keeps a bleed's haemoglobin above about 8.5, so the two units rest on the haemodynamics of an active bleed (blood pressure 86/52, pulse 124) and not on a threshold of 7 g/dl; a hepatologist confirms. The report of the albumin stay does not move with the rebleed: a later bill is history of an earlier one and adds nothing to its report.

## MEDULA rejection events

A recipe may list the MEDULA rejections of its bill (`medula_events`, empty by default): each names the line it is about by the pathway entry id (`entry_id`, an id of the pathway, or of the fault's sibling, whose expansion is the committed bill), the stage (`medula_stage`: `provizyon`, `hizmet_kayit` or `fatura_kayit`), the four-digit result code (`sonuc_kodu`), an optional error code (`hata_kodu`) and the message (`hata_mesaji`), with `basis` and `source` like every pathway entry. The builder resolves the entry to the line of the committed claim and writes one row of `medula_rejection_events.parquet`: the id `RED-<claim>-NN` (NN counts the events of the claim), the claim, patient and episode, the line (`related_line_type` and `related_line_id`; the legacy `related_procedure_code` and `related_procedure_line_id` for a procedure line) and the date of the line as `rejection_timestamp` (the loader wants it inside the claim's dates). The check `recipe.medula_event` refuses an event whose entry is not exactly one billed line of the committed bill (a line the fault takes out, a line a day, a performed-log line) and an event given twice. A clean twin carries no event: a rejection is what happened to the bill as it was submitted. The one event of the cohort is the 0901 of S10; whether a hospital would see that text for a manual haematocrit beside a hemogram is for the billing officer to confirm.

## Regenerate and check

Regenerate the files in `data/synthetic/` (the tables in `sut/claims/` and `sut/clean/`, and `cohort_registry.json`):

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.build
```

The output directory is `--out DIR` and defaults to `data/synthetic`. Verify that the committed files match an in-memory build by content (table frames and `cohort_registry.json`):

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.build --check
```

Two builds with the same Polars version write the same bytes, so a rebuild leaves every tracked file unchanged. Another Polars version may lay the same tables out in other Parquet bytes; `--check` compares by content, so it does not fail on that.

## Engine proof

Run the billing and prose-rules engine proof over the committed dataset:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.engine_proof
```

The proof is about one bill at a time: the engine runs over the claim that carries the fault (`build_bill_claim_context`), with the patient's other bills as history only (what the cross-bill rules read), and its clean twin runs over the same context with the clean bill's lines laid over it. For a patient with one bill that is the patient. This compares the loaded dataset and the in-memory clean twin against the earlier proofs (`reports/synthetic-data/2026-10-15-showcase-proofs/results/S1.json`..`S9.json`, `S10.json`, `S11.json`, `S12.json`, `S14.json`, `S22.json` and `S40.json`; a clean bill, S39, has no proof) across totals, user-facing signal multisets, report scalars, and direct detector rows. For `S8`, the full report shows `audit_exposure_try` `3584.80` while the fault record and direct detector call carry `5377.20`, because no claim line carries the incision (`Safa675/Bilun#311` item 6) so the report uses the lenient 50% second-package reading whereas the record carries the same-incision 25% reading. `S9` is the mirror: the report shows the `session_uplift` money at the strictest reading, `151.60` (30% of the second operation, same incision), while the record carries the whole gap to the clean bill, `758.01` (the clean twin bills the separate-incision rate, 50%, and the faulty bill 25%). `S22` is the same for an add-on: the report asks for 30% of the omitted polypectomy, `263.92`, while the record carries the gap to the clean bill, which bills it at 50%, `439.86`; `KNOWN_REPORT_DIVERGENCES` lists the three. On S10 the loaded bill also carries its MEDULA rejection event, and the clean twin none.

## Coverage metrics

Which of the 20 billing-report blocks and which of the 528 Wave A rule ids do the patients light up? `scripts/synthetic/cohort/coverage_metrics.py` runs the engine's patient report over a claims directory, applies the metric map of `plans/data/2026-10-20-demo-coverage-first-plan.md` section 9.2 (blocks with output, Wave A rule ids evaluated / matched / with a row, finding kinds), writes the numbers per patient and for the cohort as JSON, and prints the difference from an earlier file:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.coverage_metrics --out /tmp/before.json
# ... add a story, rebuild the cohort ...
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.synthetic.cohort.coverage_metrics --out /tmp/after.json --compare /tmp/before.json
```

`--claims-dir DIR` measures another claims folder, `--patients ID ...` only those patients. On the first bills of the 8 patients S1 to S8 it reported 9 of 20 blocks with output, 273 of 528 rule ids evaluated, 14 matched and 14 with a row (the plan's baseline); with the two outpatient bills of patient 4 the same 8 patients report 286 evaluated, 16 matched and 15 with a row, and the whole cohort 15 blocks, 288 evaluated, 19 matched and 18 with a row (patient 12 adds the blocks `lab`, `procedure_addon_uplift` and `medula_rejection_playbook` and the matched ids `GENEL CERRAHİ-2` and `tablo15_12_duplicate_test`; `tests/unit/synthetic/test_coverage_metrics.py` pins these). Limits: the report is computed from the claim tables alone, so the patient's `copay_exemption` (the EK-1/A input of the app's cache path) and the lab feed `lab_df_pl` are not passed; a block with output means the engine produced a row, not that the row is right. `--app-path` (G12b) passes both, built the way the app's cache builds them (`--clean-dir` names the clean tables, by default `sut/clean`), and adds each patient's copay status (`exempt`, `partial_exempt`, `none`), its chronic-health-report flag and the rate corrections of its copay block; documents of the two modes are not compared with each other. On the committed cohort the app path shows 12 rate corrections (2 on each of S3 to S6 and S9, 1 on S1, and S2's waiver row), five patients with an EK-1/A match and no known report (`partial_exempt`: S3 to S6 and S9), three exempt patients (S2 on EK-1/A with its report, the two emergency patients on the visit type), and the same blocks and Wave A rule ids as the claims-only run (before S2's report and its control, WP8: 13 rate corrections and six partial patients, S2 among them).

## Phase notes

- **Clinical clean tables (`sut/clean/`):** `anadata_clean.parquet` (26 rows, one per bill), `recete_clean.parquet` (24 rows), and `lab_clean.parquet` (346 rows) are populated deterministically from the showcase claims and clinical registry.
- **Lab results (`sut/clean/lab_clean.parquet`):**
  - One result set per billed EK-2B lab line (17 codes, `parametre` names the result parameter): 346 rows. Patients 5, 8, 13 and 14 have none because their bills are packages (S20's day case too), and patient 11 (the newborn, S12) has none because the lab reference ranges are adult; cultures and serology are not L-codes and have no rows.
  - Values come from `data/synthetic/reference/lab_reference.csv` by a deterministic SHA-256 draw of `patient|code|parameter|occurrence|salt` (no random seed, no clock): the central 30 to 70 percent of the sex-specific limits, or 20 to 80 percent of the range of a state.
  - The per-patient states in `data/synthetic/cohort/showcase_clinical.yaml` are judgment (clinician review in issue #309). Repeat samples inside a state are independent draws: there is no automatic trend.
  - Coherence rules: the five differential counts sum to WBC (R1); RBC = HGB·10/MCH, HCT = MCV·RBC/10, MCHC = HGB/HCT·100 (R2); creatinine is inverted from a target eGFR and eGFR is recomputed with CKD-EPI 2021 (R3); HCO3 = 0.0307·pCO2·10^(pH−6.1), BE = 0.93·(HCO3−24.4+14.8·(pH−7.4)) and SaO2 by Severinghaus from pO2 (R4).
  - `anormal_mi` is true outside the sex-specific limits of the CSV (troponin I: M 34.2, F 15.6 ng/L) and for a strip grade other than `Negatif` (R5).
