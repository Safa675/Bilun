# SC immunoglobulin, hepatitis initiation and denosumab necessary checks

## Source and implementation scope
This additive packet covers five original inventory items: C004549 (SC immunoglobulin secondary immunodeficiency); C004595/C004596 (HBV initial histology and noninvasive alternatives); C004964 (pediatric chronic HCV RNA/genotype); C006126 (denosumab glucocorticoid-related bone loss). The 89 captured context paragraphs exactly match the independently extracted October 2 official DOCX by stable key, paragraph position and active text. The source capture contains the DOCX SHA256, amendment links, numbered-item evidence and instrument-domain references.

- SC-only immunoglobulin: explicitly selected CLL requires hypogammaglobulinemia, recurrent bacterial infections and prophylactic-antibiotic failure or contraindication. MM requires the first two facts. Allogeneic HSCT requires hypogammaglobulinemia before or after transplantation. The relevant report specialist and prescriber are hematology or pediatric hematology/oncology. The report term must be positive and at most 12 months; prescribed supply must be positive and at most one month. IV administration and unselected branches remain outside this branch. The inherited B-2 rule's erroneous generic IVIG selector is repaired to source-specific SC names; its legacy ID is retained.
- HBV initial path a: documented DNA at least 2000 IU/ml and the explicit adult/2–18-year source alternative. Adult histology is HAI at least 6 or fibrosis at least 2; the noninvasive alternative requires repeated ALT above ULN at least three calendar months apart and FIB4 above 1.45 or APRI above 0.5. For the 2–18-year group, ALT above twice ULN plus HAI at least 4, or fibrosis at least 2 independent of ALT. Other initiation/continuation pathways are not rejected by this partial evaluator.
- Pediatric chronic HCV initiation: explicitly pediatric branch, positive HCV RNA and documented genotype result. Adult/other phases remain unserved by this necessary check.
- Denosumab: explicit glucocorticoid bone-loss branch in adults, osteoporosis, high fracture risk, long-term glucocorticoid therapy, related bone loss and at least one of bisphosphonate intolerance, inadequate response or renal contraindication. Other denosumab indications retain their independent assessment paths.
